<p>Hiring the right WordPress developer can make or break your project. A great developer delivers on time, writes clean code, and communicates clearly. A bad one can cost you months and thousands of dollars. Here's what to look for.</p>

<h2>1. Check Their Portfolio, Not Just Their Claims</h2>
<p>Look for real, live websites they've built. Click through them. Test the mobile experience. Check page speed. A developer's portfolio should demonstrate actual results, not just screenshots.</p>

<h2>2. Verify Their Technical Skills</h2>
<p>Ask about their experience with: WordPress themes and plugins, PHP, JavaScript, HTML/CSS, responsive design, SEO best practices, and website security. A strong developer should be comfortable discussing all of these.</p>

<h2>3. Look for Certifications and Credentials</h2>
<p>Certifications from Microsoft, Google, and HubSpot demonstrate a commitment to professional development. They're not everything, but they're a good signal of expertise.</p>

<h2>4. Ask About Their Process</h2>
<p>A professional developer has a clear process: discovery, planning, development, testing, and launch. If they can't explain their process, that's a red flag.</p>

<h2>5. Test Their Communication</h2>
<p>How quickly do they respond? Do they explain technical concepts in plain language? Clear communication is essential for a successful project.</p>

<h2>6. Check References and Reviews</h2>
<p>Ask for client references and check their Upwork, LinkedIn, or other platform reviews. Past clients are the best indicator of future performance.</p>

<h2>7. Red Flags to Watch For</h2>
<p>• No portfolio or only vague descriptions<br>• Unrealistically low prices<br>• No written contract or clear scope<br>• Promises that sound too good to be true<br>• No understanding of SEO or security</p>

<h2>8. Ask the Right Interview Questions</h2>
<p>• "How do you handle WordPress security?"<br>• "What's your approach to site speed optimization?"<br>• "How do you handle revisions and feedback?"<br>• "What happens if something breaks after launch?"<br>• "Can you show me a similar project you've built?"</p>
